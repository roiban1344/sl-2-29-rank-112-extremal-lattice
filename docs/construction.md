# From the checks to the statements

[日本語](construction.ja.md)

The scripts in `code/verify/` check identities between integer matrices. This page gives the short arguments that turn those identities into the statements marked "checked here" in [STATUS.md](../STATUS.md). Lattices are row lattices: a lattice is given by a matrix whose rows are a basis, and if the ambient form has Gram matrix A in some coordinates, a basis matrix Y in those coordinates has Gram matrix Y A Yᵀ. For a lattice X, X^# is its dual for the form in question.

## L112.03–14 inside B0 ⊥ B0

`code/verify/v2_construction.py`, function `check_wb2`.

Let b be the Gram matrix of B0 (`data/Wb2/base-gram.txt`, 56 × 56, det b = 29⁴⁸), and let E = B0 ⊥ B0, whose Gram matrix on the standard basis of ℤ¹¹² is bb = diag(b, b). The file `data/Wb2/L112.XX-basis.txt` holds an integer matrix z. The rows of z/29 are a basis of a lattice L ⊂ E ⊗ ℚ, written in the coordinates of E. The script checks the following.

1. **z bb ≡ 0 (mod 29).** The pairings of the basis of L with the basis of E are the entries of (z/29) bb, so they are integers: **L ⊂ E^#**.
2. **z bb zᵀ ≡ 0 (mod 29²), and the Gram matrix z bb zᵀ / 29² is even of determinant 1.** So L is an even integral lattice with **L^# = L**.
3. **Hence E ⊂ L.** Taking duals reverses inclusions, so L ⊂ E^# gives E = (E^#)^# ⊂ L^# = L.
4. **Index.** As E ⊂ L, det E = [L : E]² det L. Here det E = (det b)² = 29⁹⁶ and det L = 1, so **[L : E] = 29⁴⁸**. Equivalently, the basis matrix z/29 of L has |det(z/29)| = [L : E]⁻¹, that is, |det z| = 29¹¹²⁻⁴⁸ = 29⁶⁴. The script also checks |det z| = 29⁶⁴, which follows from the checks above.
5. **The published matrix.** With U = `data/Wb2/L112.XX-lll.txt` and det U = ±1, the rows of Uᵀ z / 29 are another basis of L, and its Gram matrix Uᵀ (z bb zᵀ / 29²) U is compared with `lattices/L112.XX/gram.txt`.
6. **The action of S.** Let s₁, s₂ be the two matrices of `data/Wb2/base-S.txt`, which preserve b, and Y = Uᵀ z. For the first two generators g of `lattices/L112.XX/generators.txt` the script checks g Y = Y diag(sᵢ, sᵢ). This says that the element acting diagonally on E maps the published basis of L to the integer combinations given by the rows of g. So S preserves L, and the published generators are its matrices in the published basis.

The script does not identify B0 beyond these facts. What it does not check is listed in [data/README.md](../data/README.md).

## The group generated by the generators

`code/verify/v1_action.py`.

**Exact part.** For each generator g, the script checks in integers that g G gᵀ = G, where G is the Gram matrix. As det G = 1, det g = ±1, so g is an automorphism of the lattice.

**The group is finite.** Let Γ be the group generated by these matrices. Every g ∈ Γ satisfies g G gᵀ = G, so its i-th row x satisfies x G xᵀ = Gᵢᵢ. G is positive definite, so only finitely many integer vectors x have a given norm. Hence Γ is finite.

**Reduction modulo 3 is injective on Γ.** This is classical (Minkowski 1887). Let g ∈ Γ with g ≡ I (mod 3), and suppose g ≠ I. A suitable power of g has prime order p, is still ≡ I (mod 3), and is ≠ I. Replace g by it and write g = I + 3ᵏ A with k ≥ 1 and A ≢ 0 (mod 3). Expanding (I + 3ᵏ A)ᵖ = I gives

  0 = p 3ᵏ A + C(p, 2) 3²ᵏ A² + … + 3ᵖᵏ Aᵖ.

- If p ≠ 3, every term after the first is divisible by 3ᵏ⁺¹, so p A ≡ 0 (mod 3) and A ≡ 0 (mod 3).
- If p = 3, the equation reads 3ᵏ⁺¹ A + 3²ᵏ⁺¹ A² + 3³ᵏ A³ = 0. For k ≥ 1 the last two terms are divisible by 3ᵏ⁺², so again A ≡ 0 (mod 3).

Either way this contradicts the choice of A, so g = I.

So GAP, working with the reductions modulo 3 of the generators, computes a group isomorphic to Γ. The orders of Γ and of the subgroup S generated by the first two generators are their true orders, and the isomorphism type of S is its true type. Also −I ∈ S exactly when its reduction lies in the reduction of S, because −I ≢ I (mod 3). GAP takes the orbit of a vector under S, checks that S acts faithfully on it, and compares the resulting permutation group with SL(2, 29). This comparison needs the GAP package SmallGrp.

This identifies S and checks that −I ∈ S for each distributed lattice. That −1 ∈ SL₂(29) acts as −1 on *every* extremal lattice with such an action is a different statement, part of the reduction to module types in [STATUS.md](../STATUS.md#module-types).
